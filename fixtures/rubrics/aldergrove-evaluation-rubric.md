# Aldergrove Resilience Fund
## Community Climate Adaptation Grants — Evaluation Criteria and Scoring Rubric

**Solicitation number:** ARF-CCA-2026-03
**Applies to cycle:** 2026 Community Climate Adaptation Grants

## Purpose of This Document

This rubric is provided to external review panelists scoring proposals
submitted under solicitation ARF-CCA-2026-03. It is also shared with
applicants so they understand how proposals will be evaluated. Each
panelist scores every criterion independently on a 1–5 scale using the
anchors below; criterion scores are weighted and summed to produce a
single composite score per proposal, used to rank the applicant pool for
funding recommendations to the Fund's board.

## Weighting Summary

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Significance and Fit to Priority Areas | 20% |
| Approach and Methodological Rigor | 25% |
| Feasibility and Team Capacity | 20% |
| Community and Municipal Partnership | 20% |
| Dissemination and Sustainability | 15% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

A composite score is calculated as:

```
composite = 0.20*(Significance) + 0.25*(Approach) + 0.20*(Feasibility)
          + 0.20*(Partnership) + 0.15*(Dissemination)
```

on a resulting 1.0–5.0 scale. Proposals scoring below 3.0 composite are
not recommended for funding regardless of budget availability.

## Criterion 1: Significance and Fit to Priority Areas (20%)

Does the project address a genuine gap for small or mid-sized municipal
adaptation practice, and does it fit one of the Fund's stated priority
areas (urban heat, coastal/riverine flooding, or water scarcity)?

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Directly addresses a well-documented, high-consequence gap; clear fit to a named priority area; outcome would be adopted by multiple municipalities beyond the named partners. |
| 4 | Addresses a real gap with clear priority-area fit; benefit mostly limited to the named partner municipalities. |
| 3 | Plausible significance and fit, but the gap or its consequence is asserted rather than well-supported. |
| 2 | Weak or tangential fit to the stated priority areas; significance argument is generic. |
| 1 | No clear connection to municipal climate adaptation or to a stated priority area. |

## Criterion 2: Approach and Methodological Rigor (25%)

Is the proposed method sound, appropriately scaled to the budget and
timeline, and likely to produce the claimed result?

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Method is rigorous, well-justified, and directly tied to the aims; any preliminary results are clearly described with methodology sufficient to assess credibility. |
| 4 | Method is sound and appropriate; minor gaps in justification of specific design choices. |
| 3 | Method is workable but has unaddressed limitations (e.g., sample size, control condition, validation approach). |
| 2 | Method has significant gaps that put the aims at risk; preliminary results, if claimed, are not well supported by the described methodology. |
| 1 | Method is not credible as described, or is disconnected from the stated aims. |

## Criterion 3: Feasibility and Team Capacity (20%)

Can this specific team complete this specific project within the
proposed budget and period?

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Budget, timeline, and personnel are clearly matched to the scope of work; team has demonstrated relevant prior experience. |
| 4 | Feasible overall; budget or timeline is tight in one area but not disqualifying. |
| 3 | Feasible with moderate risk; some mismatch between scope and resources requires reviewer judgment. |
| 2 | Scope appears substantially larger than the budget or timeline can support. |
| 1 | Not feasible as proposed; budget, timeline, or personnel are clearly insufficient for the stated aims. |

## Criterion 4: Community and Municipal Partnership (20%)

Is the named municipal partnership real, committed, and structured to
produce a tool the partner will actually use?

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Partner(s) are named, committed in writing, and meaningfully involved in project design, not just as a data-collection site. |
| 4 | Partner(s) are named and committed in writing; involvement is somewhat more limited (e.g., site access only). |
| 3 | Partner(s) are named and described, but commitment documentation is incomplete or ambiguous. |
| 2 | Partner(s) are named but there is no clear evidence of an actual commitment. |
| 1 | No named municipal partner, or the described partnership does not meet the eligibility requirement. |

## Criterion 5: Dissemination and Sustainability (15%)

Will the results reach practitioners beyond the named partners, and does
the project have a credible plan for the tool or workflow to outlive the
grant period?

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Concrete dissemination plan (e.g., open documentation, workshop, training) targeting a specific practitioner audience beyond named partners, with a credible sustainability path after the award ends. |
| 4 | Clear dissemination plan; sustainability path is plausible but less developed. |
| 3 | Dissemination is mentioned but not planned in detail (e.g., "will publish results" with no venue or audience specified). |
| 2 | Dissemination is an afterthought; no meaningful plan to reach practitioners beyond named partners. |
| 1 | No dissemination or sustainability plan of any kind. |

## Panelist Notes

- Score each criterion independently; do not average across criteria in
  your head before recording individual scores.
- A proposal with a strong Approach score but a weak Partnership score
  (or vice versa) is a common and expected pattern — score each
  criterion on its own merits.
- Compliance issues (missing required sections or attachments, budget
  exceeding the $75,000 ceiling) are handled separately by Fund staff
  during the eligibility screen described in
  `aldergrove-community-climate-rfp.md` and should not be re-litigated
  by panelists in the scoring above; a proposal that reaches panel
  review is assumed to have passed the compliance screen.
