# Aldergrove Resilience Fund
## Community Climate Adaptation Grants — Request for Proposals (RFP)

**Solicitation number:** ARF-CCA-2026-03
**Release date:** March 2, 2026
**Program area:** Community Climate Adaptation

## Overview

The Aldergrove Resilience Fund ("the Fund") is a private grantmaking
foundation supporting applied, community-partnered research on climate
adaptation in small and mid-sized U.S. municipalities. Through the
Community Climate Adaptation Grants program, the Fund supports one- to
two-year projects that pair academic or nonprofit research capacity with
municipal government partners to produce adaptation tools that a
resource-constrained local government can actually adopt.

This RFP solicits proposals for the 2026 grant cycle. Approximately six
awards will be made.

## Program Background and Priority Areas

The Fund was established in 2019 to address a gap its founders observed
between climate-adaptation research produced at research-intensive
universities and the operational capacity of the small planning offices
expected to use it. Most published adaptation tools assume a level of GIS
staffing, budget, and technical support that a mid-sized municipality
(population under 250,000) rarely has.

For the 2026 cycle, the Fund prioritizes proposals addressing one or more
of the following:

- **Urban and extreme heat.** Tools, sensor networks, or planning
  methods that help municipal staff identify and respond to
  neighborhood-scale heat exposure, particularly where existing
  vulnerability indices are too coarse (e.g., census-tract resolution)
  to guide site-specific interventions such as tree planting, cool-roof
  incentives, or cooling-center placement.
- **Coastal and riverine flooding.** Adaptation planning tools for
  municipalities facing recurrent nuisance flooding or long-term
  sea-level exposure, with an emphasis on tools usable without
  specialized hydrological modeling staff.
- **Water scarcity and drought.** Municipal water-system resilience
  planning in regions facing recurring or structural water stress.

Proposals must identify at least one committed municipal government
partner with a population under 250,000, and should explain concretely
how the proposed method or tool is designed for adoption by a planning
office with limited technical staff — the Fund's reviewers will weigh
this "small-office usability" test heavily regardless of topic area.

## Eligibility Criteria

Eligible applicants are:

- U.S. institutions of higher education (public or private nonprofit);
- U.S. 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations; or
- U.S. municipal or county government agencies applying directly.

The Principal Investigator must hold a faculty, research-staff, or
equivalent professional appointment at the applicant institution.
Independent researchers without an institutional affiliation are not
eligible to apply as PI, though they may participate as project
personnel under an eligible applicant.

Applicants must identify at least one committed municipal government
partner (population under 250,000) that has agreed to participate in the
project as a study site or pilot deployment location. Projects proposing
work in a single very large city (population over 250,000) are not
eligible for this program; the Fund's separate Metro Adaptation Grants
program (solicitation ARF-MAG) covers that context.

## Award Size and Period

- **Maximum award:** $75,000 total costs (direct and indirect combined)
  per project.
- **Project period:** 12 to 24 months.
- **Anticipated number of awards:** approximately six, subject to the
  quality of the applicant pool.

Budgets exceeding the $75,000 ceiling will not be considered for funding
under this solicitation regardless of scientific merit; applicants with
larger project scopes should consider phasing the work or applying to
the Fund's Metro Adaptation Grants program, which carries a higher
ceiling.

## Format Requirements

- Narrative sections (Abstract through Approach, inclusive) must not
  exceed **8 pages**, excluding budget, budget justification, references
  cited, and required attachments.
- 11-point font, Times New Roman, Garamond, or equivalent serif face.
- 1-inch margins on all sides.
- Single line spacing within paragraphs; one blank line between
  sections.
- References cited in any consistent numbered format (the Fund does not
  mandate a specific citation style).
- Submit as a single PDF file. Do not submit as multiple separate files
  or as a compressed archive.

## Required Proposal Sections

Every proposal narrative must include the following sections, in this
order, using clearly labeled headings:

1. Title
2. PI and Institutional Affiliation
3. Abstract
4. Specific Aims
5. Background and Significance
6. Approach and Methods
7. **Data Management Plan** — describing what data the project will
   collect, how it will be stored and backed up during the project
   period, and what portion (if any) will be made publicly available
   after the award period ends, consistent with the Fund's open-data
   expectations described below.
8. Budget and Budget Justification
9. References Cited

Proposals missing any of the above sections will be returned to the
applicant without review.

## Required Attachments

In addition to the narrative, applicants must submit the following as
separate attachments:

- **Letters of Institutional Commitment.** A signed letter from each
  named municipal or community partner confirming its commitment to
  participate in the project (e.g., site access, data sharing, staff
  time). Proposals naming a municipal partner without an accompanying
  commitment letter from that partner will be considered incomplete.
- **Budget Justification Narrative.** A one-page narrative explaining
  each budget line item. A budget table alone does not satisfy this
  requirement.
- **Biosketch or CV for the PI.** No more than two pages, NIH-style or
  equivalent format acceptable.
- **Current and Pending Support statement** for the PI, listing all
  other active or pending external funding.

Incomplete applications — missing any required attachment — will not
advance to panel review.

## Prohibited Content

The following are not permitted anywhere in the proposal:

- Content generated primarily by an AI writing tool and presented as the
  applicant's own analysis or original prose. Applicants may disclose
  and describe AI-assisted drafting in a brief methodological note, but
  undisclosed AI-generated narrative is grounds for disqualification.
- Fabricated, unverifiable, or non-existent citations. All cited sources
  must be independently verifiable by Fund staff.
- Preliminary results presented as though independently peer-reviewed or
  published, when they have not been.
- Budget line items for indirect costs exceeding 15% of direct costs.
- Lobbying activities or attempts to influence pending legislation, per
  the Fund's private-foundation tax status.

## Evaluation Process

Complete, eligible applications advance to a panel of external reviewers
with relevant subject-matter expertise. Panel scores are combined with
staff review of budget and compliance to produce funding
recommendations to the Fund's board. See the companion document,
**Aldergrove Resilience Fund: Evaluation Criteria and Scoring Rubric**,
for the full weighted scoring framework used by panelists.

Applicants should expect a decision within approximately 14 weeks of the
submission deadline.
