## Title
Scaffolded Fraction Reasoning: A Grade 4-5 Intervention Study in Rural Districts

## PI/Institution
Dr. Ronan H. Kestrel, College of Education, Ferncliff Institute of Learning Sciences

## Abstract
Persistent gaps in fraction reasoning at the upper elementary level
predict downstream difficulty with algebra and proportional reasoning,
and rural districts frequently lack access to the intensive coaching
supports that anchor most published interventions. This project
adapts an existing classroom-tested fraction-reasoning sequence for
delivery under realistic rural constraints — limited coaching time,
mixed-grade classrooms, and paraprofessional support in place of
dedicated math specialists — and evaluates it across twelve
participating Grade 4 and Grade 5 classrooms in four rural school
districts. The primary outcome is student performance on a
validated fraction-reasoning assessment administered pre- and
post-intervention.

## Aims
- **Aim 1:** Adapt the existing "Scaffolded Fraction Reasoning" (SFR)
  sequence for a lower-coaching-intensity delivery model appropriate
  to rural district staffing.
- **Aim 2:** Pilot the adapted sequence across twelve classrooms
  spanning four rural districts, with pre/post assessment of fraction
  reasoning.
- **Aim 3:** Produce a freely available teacher-facing implementation
  guide, together with a short-form assessment tool districts can
  adopt independently.

## Background & Prior Work
Difficulty with fraction magnitude and equivalence in Grades 4-5 is
one of the strongest classroom-observable predictors of later algebra
performance, a finding replicated across multiple longitudinal
studies [1]. The Scaffolded Fraction Reasoning sequence, developed
over the last decade in suburban laboratory-school settings, has
consistently produced meaningful post-intervention gains — but its
published implementations rely on weekly coaching visits and
dedicated math specialists that most rural districts cannot staff [2].

The methodological question this project takes up is whether the
active ingredients of SFR survive a lower-touch implementation. As
Priestley observes, "the sequence's efficacy appears to depend more
on the fidelity of the task progression than on the intensity of
adult coaching, though direct evidence for this claim remains
limited" [2]. Preliminary work in a single district has suggested
that a paraprofessional-supported model may recover a substantial
share of the coaching-intensive gains, but no multi-district study
has yet tested this systematically [3].

## Approach
Twelve classrooms across four rural districts will be recruited via
existing regional education-service-agency partnerships. Each
classroom will receive the adapted SFR sequence over an eight-week
window, with fidelity monitored via weekly teacher logs and
biweekly video sampling. Pre- and post-assessment will use the
Fraction Concepts Inventory (FCI), a validated instrument [1]. In
a preceding single-district pilot, students receiving the adapted
sequence gained an average of 0.58 standard deviations on the FCI,
compared with a 0.19 standard-deviation gain in a matched
comparison classroom over the same window.
