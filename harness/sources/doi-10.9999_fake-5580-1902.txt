Across the four multi-year implementations reviewed here, the sequence's
efficacy appears to depend more on the fidelity of the task progression
than on the intensity of adult coaching, though direct evidence for this
claim remains limited. The three high-fidelity, low-coaching sites
produced post-intervention gains within 0.1 standard deviations of the
two high-fidelity, high-coaching sites, while the two low-fidelity sites
— regardless of coaching intensity — produced substantially smaller
gains. This pattern is suggestive rather than conclusive: the sample of
sites is small, participating districts self-selected into the study,
and no site was randomized to a coaching-intensity condition. A
pre-registered comparison across matched districts remains the most
credible next step. We therefore treat the pattern as a hypothesis that
motivates further work, not as an established finding about the
coaching-vs-fidelity tradeoff.
